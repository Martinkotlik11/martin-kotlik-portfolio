Spot Welding, Riveting & Bonding - Joint Strength Tests

Three ways of joining 1 mm DC01 steel sheet, compared on the same tensile machine. We sized each specimen from design rules, varied the process parameters, broke the joints and looked at where and why they failed.

KEY RESULTS

What the tests showed

Figures taken from the report. Click any image to enlarge.

01

Current matters far more than weld time

We welded single-spot specimens at 20, 40, 60 and 80 % of the machine's 14 kA, each with 5, 10 and 15 cycles. Strength stays below 200 MPa at the two lowest currents, reaches about 270 MPa at 8,400 A and levels off around 320 MPa at 11,200 A.

At full current, the number of cycles barely changes the result. That fits the Joule heating law, E = R·I²·t: energy grows with the square of the current but only linearly with time. We kept 11,200 A and 5 cycles - the strongest joint with the least heat put into the part.

Tensile strength against current · against number of cycles

02

Too much heat weakens the area around the weld

We cut a weld in half, polished it to a mirror finish and etched it with nital. Away from the weld the grains are still elongated from rolling. Closer in, they recrystallise, and right next to the nugget they have grown coarse.

Coarse grains and a sharp change in microstructure make that ring the weakest point. It explains why the longest, hottest welds broke at the edge of the nugget rather than in the sheet: more energy does not always mean a stronger joint.

Micrograph of the weld zone · metallurgy notes from the lab

03

Where the second spot goes changes how it fails

We sized two-spot specimens from the design rules: edge distance 2e + 4 mm, spot pitch 20 mm (at least 10e + 10 so the current does not shunt through the first spot), giving a 42 × 17 mm overlap.

With both spots in line with the load, the sheet itself broke and the welds held. Side by side, the joint carried a higher peak load, around 10 kN, but it was the welds that tore out.

Two spots in series (sheet failure) · two spots in parallel (weld failure)

04

Removing the bending moment

Riveted joints were sized with 4 mm rivets, a 15 mm pitch and 7.5 mm edge distance. In a simple lap joint the two sheets are offset, so the specimen twists under load and the rivets fail first. Rivets in line with the load did better than rivets side by side.

To get rid of the twist, we riveted three sheets in a sandwich and drilled them together to remove play. The rivets then held and the sheet broke first, at about 4.6 kN.

Sandwich riveted specimen after the test · its force-displacement curve

05

Surface preparation and cure temperature

Bonded joints used Araldite 2011, a two-part epoxy. From a 25 MPa shear strength, the overlap needed for the sheet to break before the joint came out at 14.8 mm. Specimens were degreased with acetone, some were scratched to roughen them, and they were cured either for 10 h at room temperature or 45 min at 60 °C.

Curing at 60 °C doubled the peak load, and the scratched, heat-cured joint was the strongest at about 8.4 kN. All joints failed by peeling from the ends of the overlap, caused by a small misalignment in the grips. A sandwich specimen reduced that effect, as it did for the rivets.

Bonded specimens: 20 °C, 20 °C scratched, 60 °C, 60 °C scratched

PROJECT OVERVIEW

Size, make, test, compare

Size

Used the design rules for each process to size the specimens: electrode and spot diameter, edge distance and spot pitch for welding; rivet pitch and edge distance for riveting; overlap length for bonding.

Make

Welded, riveted and bonded the specimens in the workshop, changing one parameter at a time: current and weld time, joint layout, surface preparation and cure temperature.

Test

Measured each specimen and pulled it to failure on a tensile machine, recording force against displacement. Prepared a cross-section of the best weld for metallography.

Compare

Spot welding gave the highest strength. Riveting and bonding came close once the geometry was improved: in the sandwich specimens the joint stopped being the weak point.

Context: four-person team, joining processes lab (RESA), Arts et Métiers Lille. Report written in French.
